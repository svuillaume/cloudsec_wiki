FortiCNAPP Tenant Decommissioning

SOC 2-aligned offboarding — one timeline, three trigger paths, two notifications. Written the way the field will actually use it.

SV
Sam Vuillaume — Consulting Systems Expert, Cloud & AI Security · Fortinet
May 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Why this process exists

When a FortiCNAPP POC ends or a customer chooses not to renew, the tenant has to come down — cleanly, predictably, and in line with our SOC 2 controls. This document captures the three real-world ways a decommission gets triggered, the single timeline that applies to every one of them, and the notifications expected at each milestone.

This is written the way the field will actually use it: short, scannable, no flow diagrams that need a key.

The short version

"Tenant disabled within one business day. Data deleted within thirty. Field notified at both ends. That's the whole process."

The timeline

Regardless of how a decommission is triggered, what happens after the CS ticket is opened is identical. There are only three milestones that matter.

Decommission timeline
T‑0
CS ticket opened. The clock starts here.
T+1 BD
Tenant disabled. Access and ingestion stopped. Field notified by email.
T+30
Data deleted per SOC 2 control. Confirmation email sent. Ticket closed.

Between T+1 and T+30 there is a retention hold — the tenant is dark, but the data still exists. If something was flagged in error, recovery is possible during this window. Once T+30 hits, deletion is final.

The three trigger paths

The downstream timeline is the same in every case. What changes is who originates the request and who opens the CS ticket.

Path 1
Customer-initiated

The customer files a CS ticket directly asking for tenant disablement or deletion. PreSales is CC'd for visibility but is not in the critical path. CS validates, disables, and deletes on the standard timeline.

Path 2
Fortinet representative–initiated

A Fortinet representative — Sales, CSM, SE, or Renewals — tells PreSales that a POC ended or the customer did not renew. PreSales opens the CS ticket on the representative's behalf, same business day. This is the most common path.

Path 3
Monthly reconciliation

At month-end, PreSales pulls the list of contracts that expired that month from FortiCare and asks CSM, SE, and Renewals leads to confirm renewed vs. churned. For every confirmed churn, PreSales opens a CS ticket. This is the systemic backstop that catches anything Paths 1 and 2 missed.

Notifications — who hears what, and when

Notification is the part of this process the field cares about most. The table below is the entire policy.

Event From To
CS ticket opened PreSales Lead CS, AE, CSM, SE, Renewals
Tenant disabled (T+1 BD) Customer Support PreSales → field stakeholders
Data deletion complete (T+30) Customer Support PreSales → field stakeholders
Monthly churn reconciliation PreSales Lead CSM, SE, Renewals leads

Field cheat sheet

If you are an SE, CSM, AE, or Renewals representative — this is the short version. Save it, screenshot it, pin it.

◆  For the Field — Four questions, four answers
When do I act?

POC ended without conversion, renewal lapsed and the account is confirmed churned, or the customer asked you to take their tenant down. In any of these cases — same day, tell PreSales.

Who do I tell?

PreSales Lead (FortiCNAPP). One intake point. Email or ticket — not a Slack DM you'll forget about.

What does PreSales need from me?

Account name, FortiCNAPP tenant identifier (URL, account ID, or region), and a reason: POC ended, no-renew, customer request, other.

What timeline can I quote internally?

Tenant disabled the same or next business day. Data deleted within 30 days. You will be emailed at both milestones.

A few things worth knowing

The 30-day window is intentional. It exists so the field can catch and reverse a mistake — if someone flagged an account as churned when they shouldn't have, recovery is possible right up until deletion executes. After T+30, it is irrecoverable.

Suspension is not decommissioning. A tenant suspended for non-payment still exists; data is preserved while billing is resolved. Decommissioning is terminal: disable, hold, delete.

Integrations stop on disablement. Outbound feeds to Slack, Jira, ServiceNow, and SIEM forwarders stop sending data the moment the tenant goes dark. Inbound API calls return auth errors. Advise customers to remove credentials on their side as a hygiene step.

Customer trust matters. Their data is a liability the moment a contract ends. We do not reuse former customer data for demos, screenshots, or marketing. Retention exists for recovery — nothing else.

Closing thought

Decommissioning a tenant is one of those operational disciplines that is invisible when it works and very loud when it does not. The goal of this process is to keep it invisible. One timeline. Three trigger paths. Two notifications. Thirty days.

If you have an account that needs to come down today, you already know what to do.
